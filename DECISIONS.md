# Decision log

Gameplay and project decisions made while building NEO overnight without being able to ask. Each entry says what was chosen, the alternatives, and why, so you can review and overturn any of them. Newest at the bottom.

| # | Decision | Alternatives considered | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Plain JavaScript, no build step, no runtime dependencies**, classic `<script>` files sharing one global scope, as merryfolk does | ES modules, a bundler (Vite), TypeScript, a game engine (Phaser) | Matches merryfolk so the same CI shape works; runs from `file://`; nothing to install to play; tests only need `puppeteer-core` |
| 2 | **Room-based dungeon** (Isaac-style grid of rooms, doors seal until the room is cleared). Floors are short and mostly linear (4–5 rooms) with a one-room side branch for the cache. **You can walk back within a floor**, but never to an earlier floor or the apartment | One continuous Diablo-style cave; rooms that lock behind you | Rooms suit a portrait screen and pace a 5-minute run. "Can't go back" is read as no return to the apartment or earlier floors; locking every room behind you would make the side-branch cache impossible to reach |
| 3 | **Stand still to attack** (auto-fire at the nearest enemy when not moving, as in Archero), with a setting to fire while moving at reduced damage | Twin-stick (two joysticks), tap-to-shoot, always auto-fire | The only scheme that's truly one-thumb and still has skill: positioning versus shooting. Twin-stick needs two thumbs in portrait |
| 4 | **5 floors, the 5th is a boss** (about 45 s a floor) | 3 longer floors; 10 tiny floors | Hits the 5-minute target with room for a patch choice between floors |
| 5 | **No XP and levels.** Power comes from loot plus one perk ("patch") pick per floor | Diablo-style XP and level-ups | Loot is the Diablo half and patches are the roguelite half; level-up pop-ups every 20 s would crowd a 5-minute run |
| 6 | **Four item slots: Exploit (weapon), Coat, Shades, Implant** | Diablo's ten slots | Small enough to compare on a phone in a second, and they match Neo's iconic look |
| 7 | **The game pauses while a loot compare card is open** | Auto-equip; a non-blocking toast | Lets you make the Diablo "is this better?" decision without dying; an auto-scrap setting removes the friction for common drops |
| 8 | **The dream twist is never stated or shown.** Every run, won or lost, ends by cutting back to the *identical* opening shot: Neo asleep at the CRT, *Wake up, Neo…* typing. He never wakes from the dungeon; he only "wakes" into it. Tells: the clock is always 3:14; process names `hypnos.d` and `rem_cycle`; a mirror showing his eyes closed; the white-rabbit room's window into his apartment, where he sleeps | An explicit "it was a dream" reveal; showing him jolt awake after death (an earlier draft) | You asked that the player not know. Showing him wake up would have implied the dungeon was the dream and the apartment real; the review pointed out that he never woke up at all |
| 9 | **The apartment is the hub.** The desk terminal is the permanent-upgrade shop and the door starts a run | A separate menu screen for upgrades | Keeps the "wake up at the desk" scene meaningful on every run instead of a one-off intro |
| 10 | **Uses Matrix names directly** ("Wake up, Neo", the Agent, the Oracle, the white rabbit) as you asked | Renamed knock-offs | You asked for it, and it's a personal fan project. **Worth reviewing:** the GitHub Pages site is public, and the names and lines belong to Warner Bros. If you ever share it widely, consider renaming |
| 11 | **Run length set by room count:** floors of 4–5 small rooms with 3–7 enemies each, aiming at about 45 s per floor. **There's been no human playtest:** numbers come from reasoning plus a headless bot's measured runs (see `docs/BALANCE.md` once written) | 6–8 rooms a floor (first draft) | The reviewer worked out that the first draft would run 8–12 minutes |
| 12 | **Phase (dash) is a flick gesture on the joystick**, plus an optional PHASE button. FOCUS (Bullet Time) is a button | A dash button only | Pressing a button means lifting your thumb off the joystick, which stops you and starts firing, so you could never dash while running with one thumb |
| 13 | **The joystick only works in the bottom control deck**, never on the room view | A joystick anywhere on screen | Your thumb never covers the action |
| 14 | **Auto-scrap Commons by default**; items for an empty slot are always equipped at once | Every drop opens a card | Fewer interruptions in a 5-minute run; you can turn it off in Settings |
| 15 | **The run timer stops while paused or while a card is open** | Wall-clock time | Fair for the best-time record |
| 16 | **Depth (difficulty tiers 1–5)** unlocks at the terminal after your first win: tougher enemies, better loot and more Data | Nothing after a win | Gives the meta a long-term goal, like Hades' heat |
| 17 | **Font: VT323 bundled in the repo** (open font licence) | Google Fonts; pixel font only | Works offline and from `file://`, and the look stays consistent |
| 18 | **PWA manifest and icons, but no service worker** | A full offline PWA | A service worker can keep serving an old build after a deploy; the game is small and loads fast anyway |
| 19 | **New public GitHub repo `doctorallen/Neo`**, Pages built by GitHub Actions; deploys only after the tests pass (same `pages.yml` shape as merryfolk) | A private repo | Free GitHub Pages needs a public repo; it matches merryfolk |
| 20 | **Kept, despite a scope-cut suggestion:** Daily Dream, Continue (a floor checkpoint, because phones kill background tabs), the Zero-Day codex, the `agent` mini-boss (it reuses the boss's first phase), 6 exploits, 6 enemy types. **Cut:** the *Mirrored* elite modifier | The reviewer suggested cutting most of these to protect polish | Each is small given the shared systems, and they add replay value; I'll cut anything that turns out buggy rather than ship it broken, and log it here |
| 21 | **Audio defaults:** music 50%, effects 80%, separate sliders; reduced flashing and no shake by default when the system asks for reduced motion | One volume control | A phone can't mute the music without muting effects otherwise |
| 22 | **Adopted almost every must-fix from three game-designer reviews** (summarised in `docs/REVIEWS.md`): enemy-bullet readability rules, telegraph minimums, on-screen caps, stat caps, a reworked luck formula, rebalanced exploits, a reordered cold open | Building the first spec as written | They caught several balance breaks (endless Bullet Time, permanent phase, a luck stat that did nothing) and a layout that wouldn't fit Safari |
| 23 | **Loot can't be inspected mid-fight**: drops show their name until the room is clear, then walking over them opens the card | Pausing for a card mid-fight | Stops a dodge from accidentally opening a menu, and stops the card being used as a free pause |
| 24 | **Caches offer "pick 1 of 2"** (3 with the Scavenger patch) instead of dropping two items | Two free items | Fewer cards and a real decision |
| 25 | **The final boss rewards Data and unlocks a new Zero-Day into the drop pool** instead of dropping an item | Dropping a Zero-Day after the last fight | An item after the final fight could never be used |
| 26 | **Zero-Days: 6 in the pool from the start, and 6 more bought ("decompiled") at the terminal**, or unlocked by beating the boss | All 12 from the start | Gives Data a use once the upgrades are bought, and gives later runs new toys |
| 27 | **The word "dream" never appears to the player.** *Daily Dream* became **Daily Trace** | Keeping the name | It would give the twist away |
| 28 | **Residual self-image:** Neo wears a plain shirt in the apartment, and the long coat and shades only appear once he's through the door | The coat everywhere | A quiet tell (as in the film's Construct) that the dungeon isn't waking life |
| 29 | **Bullet Time lets you fire while moving at full damage** | Stand still to fire, as at other times | It's the "Neo moment"; standing still inside it wastes it |
| 30 | **"Compiling":** after 1.2 s of standing still, +25% fire rate until you move | No ramp | Sharpens the stand-or-move tension that the controls are built on |
| 31 | **Difficulty target: a fresh player (as the "human" bot) wins about 40–45% of first runs**, and wins most runs after 2–3 runs of upgrades. Depth 3–5 is the long-term challenge (Depth 5 with all upgrades: about 25%) | Harder early runs and slower progress | A five-minute phone game should pay off quickly; see `docs/BALANCE.md`. **The first thing to tune after you play:** upgrade strength (`T.upgrades`, `computeStats`) and enemy damage (`T.enemyDmg`) |
| 32 | **Data is banked at every floor checkpoint**, not only at the end of a run | Banking only at the end | Phones kill background tabs; you never lose more than one floor's Data |
| 33 | **The final boss has no fixed timer and no enrage** | An enrage timer | Keeps a five-minute run from becoming a stressful race; the code storm in his last phase already forces movement |
| 34 | **Routine loot news (equipped / scrapped) goes to the terminal feed in the deck**; only Epic and Zero-Day finds get a toast over the room | Toasts for everything | The QA playtest found toasts covering the bottom of the room mid-fight |
| 35 | **Known limits, left for you to judge:** on a desktop the room draws at 2× in a 480 px column with the touch deck still showing; on the smallest phones (320 px wide) feed lines are cut short; the mirror and the rabbit-room window are only a few pixels across (deliberately subtle) | A desktop-specific layout | The game is built for phones in portrait; desktop is playable but secondary |
